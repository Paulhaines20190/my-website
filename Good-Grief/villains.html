Peanuts rarely needs a traditional villain. Switch between recurring rivals and the characters whose behaviour crosses into bullying.

Recurring rivals

ARCH-RIVAL 01 🌳

The Kite-Eating Tree

Charlie Brown’s most persistent natural enemy has an appetite for kites and perfect timing.

Open case file

Known weakness: None confirmed. Best approached without a kite.

Why it matters: The tree turns repeated frustration into a funny symbol of persistence.

RIVAL 02 ✈️

The Red Baron

Snoopy’s ultimate imaginary archenemy waits beyond the doghouse whenever the World War I Flying Ace takes flight.

Open case file

Battlefield: The skies of Snoopy’s imagination.

Why it matters: This unseen rival transforms an ordinary doghouse into an epic aerial adventure.

ANTAGONIST 03 🏈

Lucy and the Football Trick

Lucy is a friend rather than a conventional villain, but she becomes Charlie Brown’s recurring antagonist whenever she pulls the football away.

Open case file

Warning sign: Absolute confidence that this time will be different.

Why it matters: The running joke balances trust, optimism and inevitable disappointment.

MENACE 04 🐾

World War II Cat / The Neighbor’s Cat

The unseen neighborhood cat wages destructive turf wars with Snoopy and Woodstock while remaining just out of view.

Open case file

Threat level: Best judged from a safe distance—preferably away from the doghouse.

Why it matters: Keeping the cat unseen makes the menace seem larger, fiercer and funnier.

Known bullies

BULLY 01 🎯

Joe Agate

An expert marble player who takes advantage of Rerun until Charlie Brown challenges him and wins the marbles back.

Open case file

Method: Uses his experience and confidence to intimidate a younger player.

Why it matters: Joe gives Charlie Brown a rare opportunity to stand up to a bully and succeed.

BULLY 02 ⚾

Thibault

A short-tempered and argumentative member of Peppermint Patty’s baseball team who directs hostile behaviour at others.

Open case file

Known for: Picking fights and making chauvinistic comments toward Marcie.

Why it matters: Thibault shows how aggression can disrupt a team and test its friendships.

FILM BULLIES 03 🛶

The Camp Bullies

A domineering trio determined to defeat Charlie Brown and his friends throughout the camp competitions and river race.

Open case file

Tactics: Cheating, intimidation and sending Brutus after Snoopy and Woodstock.

Why it matters: The trio creates a direct team-versus-team conflict in Race for Your Life, Charlie Brown .

COURT RIVAL 04 🎾

Molly Volley

A fiercely competitive tennis player whose aggressive court manner overwhelms Schroeder and challenges Snoopy.

Open case file

Tactics: Loud intimidation, relentless competitiveness and a win-at-all-costs attitude.

Why it matters: Molly turns a friendly doubles match into a comic test of patience and teamwork.

UNFRIENDLY PEERS 05 💬

Violet Gray & Patty

Early neighborhood peers who often patronize, exclude or behave snobbishly toward Charlie Brown.

Open case file

Tactics: Teasing, social exclusion and dismissive comments.

Why it matters: Their behaviour represents the everyday peer pressure and rejection Charlie Brown repeatedly faces.

ACCOMPLICE 06 🐈

Brutus

The camp bullies’ aggressive pet bobcat, used to terrorize Snoopy and Woodstock during the wilderness adventure.

Open case file

Territory: The camp and its dangerous river race.

Why it matters: Brutus turns the bullies’ threats into a direct physical danger.
